## Q1. Exact legal text: Annex III 1.1.9, 1.2.1(f), Annex IV, Article 3 "substantial modification", Article 10(3), Article 50, Annex I

### Takeaway
1.1.9 imposes five obligations (no hazard from connected/remote devices; protect signal/data hardware and collect evidence of legitimate/illegitimate interventions in it; identify and protect safety-critical software and data; identify installed safety software and provide it on demand; collect evidence of interventions in or modifications of software/configuration). 1.2.1(f) requires the tracing log of intervention data and uploaded safety-software versions to stay enabled for **five years** after each upload, accessible only on reasoned request from a national authority. The Regulation applies from **20 January 2027**; penalty rules must be notified by **14 October 2026** per the EUR-Lex text (several secondary sources say 20 October).

### Cited Findings
- 1.1.9 is a new EHSR compared with Directive 2006/42/EC and is treated as a cybersecurity matter; it sits with 1.2.1 under "cybersecurity and digital aspects" — [EUR-Lex summary: machinery safety requirements](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/machinery-safety-requirements.html); [Festo blog](https://www.festo.com/ie/en/e/blog/in-practice/machinery-regulation-eu-2023-1230-id_4168408).
- Content of 1.1.9 as summarised by consultants: "the connection of another device or a remote access must not trigger a hazardous situation. Safety-critical hardware components must be protected against unintended or intentional corruption and must make interventions demonstrable. Safety-critical software and data must be identified as such and protected"; "The machine must identify the installed safety-relevant software and make this information easily accessible at any time"; the machine "must collect evidence of legitimate and illegitimate interventions in safety-related hardware and software" — [Checkmate Experts](https://www.checkmate.expert/en/maschinenverordnung-cybersicherheit.html); [vamisec](https://vamisec.com/en/maschinenverordnung); [Jama Software](https://www.jamasoftware.com/blog/eu-machinery-regulation/); [NTT Data Belgium](https://be.nttdata.com/insights/blog/gear-up-for-change-the-eu-machinery-regulation).
- 1.2.1(f) (fragment confirmed by search against the EUR-Lex page): the tracing log "…enabled for five years after such upload, exclusively to demonstrate the conformity of the machinery or related product with this Annex further to a reasoned request from a competent national authority" — [EUR-Lex 2023/1230](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj/eng). A secondary guide loosens this to "accessible for up to five years after the upload" — [SOPX OEM checklist](https://sopx.io/insights/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230/) (prefer the EUR-Lex wording).
- 1.2.1(g) (self-evolving behaviour): data on safety-related decision-making must be recorded and retained for **1 year** after collection — [4m4.it longform](https://4m4.it/longforms/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230-cyber-physical-safety/).
- Article 3(16) "substantial modification" (confirmed in substance by search against EUR-Lex): a modification "by physical or digital means" after placing on the market, "not foreseen or planned by the manufacturer", which affects safety by creating a new hazard or increasing an existing risk and requires either (a) the addition of guards or protective devices necessitating modification of the existing safety control system, or (b) additional protective measures for stability or mechanical strength. Recitals exclude repair and maintenance that do not affect compliance — [EUR-Lex 2023/1230](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj/eng); [docks.io](https://docks.io/en/maschinenverordnung/wesentliche-veraenderung/); [GT Engineering](https://www.gt-engineering.it/en/insights/machinery-directive/machinery-substantial-modifications/); [eshield.pl](https://eshield.pl/en/knowledge/substantial-modification-machinery/).
- Article 18: whoever carries out a substantial modification "shall be considered a manufacturer for the purposes of this Regulation" and takes on the Article 10 obligations for the modified machinery — [EUR-Lex 2023/1230](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj/eng); [getreadycompliance.eu](https://getreadycompliance.eu/substantial-modification-ce-marking/).
- Article 50(1): penalties must be "effective, proportionate and dissuasive" and may include criminal penalties for serious infringements. Article 50(2): notification deadline **14 October 2026** in the EUR-Lex text; secondary sources give 20 October 2026 — [EUR-Lex 2023/1230](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj/eng); contradicted (20 Oct) by [Nemko guide](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/eu-machinery-regulation) and [fabrico.io](https://www.fabrico.io/blog/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230/).
- Application date 20 January 2027, Directive 2006/42/EC repealed the same day; no grace period for machines placed on the market from that date — [EU-OSHA](https://osha.europa.eu/en/legislation/directive/regulation-20231230eu-machinery); [TÜV Rheinland](https://www.tuv.com/world/en/new-machinery-regulation-eu-2023-1230.html); [Pilz](https://www.pilz.com/en-US/support/law-standards-norms/manufacturer-machine-operators/machinery-regulation).
- Annex I is split: Part A = always third-party conformity assessment (Module B+C, H or G); Part B = self-certification (Module A) permitted only if the relevant harmonised standards are applied in full, otherwise notified body — [Rockwell guide (PDF)](https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/sp/oem-sp123_-en-p.pdf); [Checkmate Experts](https://www.checkmate.expert/en/maschinenverordnung-cybersicherheit.html). Part A has 6 categories and Part B 19 — [Nemko guide](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/eu-machinery-regulation). Part A includes "safety components with fully or partially self-evolving behaviour using machine learning approaches ensuring safety functions" and machinery embedding such systems — [cemarkingauthority.com](https://www.cemarkingauthority.com/post/introduction-to-the-new-eu-machinery-safety-regulation-2023-1230). Sources conflict on whether removable transmission devices/guards, vehicle lifts and portable impact machinery are in Part A — [sopx.io](https://sopx.io/insights/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230/) vs [ATIC](https://www.atic-ts.com/european-new-machiney-regulation/).
- Annex IV Part A (technical documentation) must contain at least a complete description of the machinery and its intended use, risk-assessment documentation (list of applicable EHSRs, protective measures, residual risks), drawings and circuit diagrams, references to harmonised standards applied; retention 10 years — [tekom (PDF)](https://www.tekom.de/fileadmin/tekom.eu/Downloads/Member_Area/New_Machinery_Regulation_final.pdf); [onix.com guide](https://onix.com/guides/eu-machinery-regulation); [soply.io](https://soply.io/resources/articles/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230-documentation). A Dutch law firm states that Annex IV requires source code or programming logic be available to demonstrate conformity — [MAAK Advocaten](https://www.maak-law.com/product-compliance-netherlands/new-machinery-regulation-eu-2023-1230/) (not verified against the OJ text in this session).

### Inferences
- From prior knowledge of the OJ text (to verify): Article 10(3) second subparagraph says the source code or programmed logic included in the technical documentation shall be made available to competent national authorities upon reasoned request where necessary to check compliance with Annex III, and Annex IV Part A lists "the source code or programmed logic of the safety-related software" among the items to be produced on reasoned request. The search summaries could not confirm this; one source attributes Article 10(3) only to the 10-year retention duty. Treat as plausible, unconfirmed.
- Sales implication: the two "collect evidence of a legitimate or illegitimate intervention" sentences in 1.1.9 (hardware and software/configuration) plus 1.2.1(f)'s five-year enabled log are the direct legal hook for an intervention log; 1.2.1(a) ("reasonably foreseeable malicious attempts from third parties", per the consultant summaries) is the hook for a per-write authorisation gateway. The gateway alone covers only the "protection" half; the log is what makes 1.1.9 §2/§5 and 1.2.1(f) demonstrable (consistent with the gap already noted in `partners/POSITIONING_GAPS.md` A1).
- Annex I matters for the vendor only indirectly: standard packaging/food/wood machines are mostly Annex I Part B or outside Annex I, so most 10–250-employee builders self-certify (Module A) — the evidence pack goes into their own technical file, not to a notified body, **unless** they lack a fully applied harmonised standard for a Part B machine.

### Gaps
- Verbatim text of 1.1.9 (all five paragraphs), 1.2.1 (a)–(g), Article 10(3), Article 54 and Annex IV Part A (a)–(k): EUR-Lex unreachable; reconstruct from the OJ before use.
- Whether "20 October 2026" (user's premise) or "14 October 2026" (EUR-Lex per search) is the Article 50(2) notification date: EUR-Lex wins until a corrigendum is found.
- Exact Annex I Part B item numbers for "logic units to ensure safety functions" and "software ensuring safety functions" (sources disagree on numbering, 17–19).

## Q2. FprEN 50742 — stage, SRSL levels, logging, authentication, integrity, secure boot, scope exclusion

### Takeaway
As of the latest sources (IBF page amended 5 Oct 2026), EN 50742 exists only as **FprEN 50742:2026 (Final Draft) at CENELEC Approval/Formal Vote stage**; no source confirms a vote result, publication, or OJ citation, and a June 2026 German source states that listing in the OJ before 20 Jan 2027 "is not yet established". The draft defines Safety-Related Security Levels (SRSL 0–3) per safety function, requires automatic, tamper-resistant intervention logs retained ≥ 5 years with documented access/interpretation, entity authentication from SRSL1 (unique at SRSL3), integrity verification of exchanged information from SRSL1, and mandatory secure boot at SRSL3; an alternative route maps to IEC 62443-3-3 SL-C2.

### Cited Findings
- Stage: "now available as FprEN 50742:2026 (Final Draft), following public consultation"; CENELEC project "at the Approval Stage, i.e. the final voting phase"; after a successful vote the standard "can be published and then adopted nationally as EN 50742". Page metadata shows last amendment 2026-10-05 — [IBF Solutions](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-standard-pren-50742-protection-against-corruption). Vote result/deadline **not found**.
- Draft lineage: prEN 50742:2025 and oSIST prEN 50742:2026 drafts listed by a standards reseller — [iTeh prEN 50742:2025](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/clc/36b30e54-1944-4a79-a720-790152a8b083/pren-50742-2025); [iTeh oSIST prEN 50742:2026](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/sist/07a63742-502d-4d13-bdb9-17a86e07b9f8/osist-pren-50742-2026). German national draft "DIN EN 50742, Norm-Entwurf März 2026" (DIN Media) — cited via [cyber-regulierung.de](https://www.cyber-regulierung.de/mvo-security-pflicht/). Draft of a harmonised standard "erschienen" since January 2026 — [IBF seminar page](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/seminare-und-wissen/technische-spezialseminare/web-seminar-schutz-gegen-korrumpierung-nach-maschinenverordnung-die-neue-en-50742).
- Status as of 23 June 2026 (update date): "the timeline for EN 50742 remains a planning factor, not a guaranteed publication"; whether EN 50742 will be listed in the OJ as a harmonised standard in time for January 2027 "steht derzeit noch nicht fest" — [cyber-regulierung.de](https://www.cyber-regulierung.de/mvo-security-pflicht/).
- Formal Vote procedure (generic): final draft sent to national committees, weekly electronic voting; a negative vote leads to a committee decision on next steps — [CENELEC BOSS Formal Vote](https://boss.cenelec.eu/homegrowndeliverables/EN/Pages/FV); [CEN BOSS](https://boss.cen.eu/developingdeliverables/pages/en/pages/fv/).
- Two routes: "Alternative A – EN 50742 based approach: an Attack Potential is determined. In combination with the Severity Level derived from the ISO 12100 risk assessment, a Safety-Related Security Requirement (SRSL) is defined"; Approach B: comply with IEC 62443-3-3 at **SL-C2** for identification & authentication control, use control, system integrity and resource availability — [cyber-regulation.com](https://cyber-regulation.com/article/en-50742-protection-against-machine-tampering); [cyber-regulierung.de](https://www.cyber-regulierung.de/eu-cybersecurity-regulierung/maschinenverordnung/).
- Authentication by level: "SRSL0 · None; SRSL1 · Entities shall be authenticated; SRSL2 · Entities shall be authenticated; SRSL3 · Entities shall be uniquely authenticated" — [cyber-regulation.com](https://cyber-regulation.com/article/en-50742-protection-against-machine-tampering).
- Integrity: at SRSL1 "The integrity of exchanged information shall be verified" — [cyber-regulation.com](https://cyber-regulation.com/article/en-50742-protection-against-machine-tampering).
- Secure boot: "SRSL3: A secure boot process shall be implemented (e.g. cryptographic signature verification with trusted roots, rollback or safe state upon verification failure)"; defined as ensuring "the machine only starts software that has been digitally signed by the manufacturer" — [cyber-regulation.com](https://cyber-regulation.com/article/en-50742-protection-against-machine-tampering); [Digitale Faszination](https://digitale-faszination.de/en/2026/01/11/draft-en-50742/).
- Logging scope: "Logging (tracing logs), evidence collection, storage duration and protection of logs, and anti-tampering measures are required to detect and investigate interventions"; events: "changes to safety parametrization or configuration parameters, changes to embedded software and application software, parametrization of HMI's (if it can create hazards), and software relevant for displaying safety instructions… Collect digital evidence of actions that changes/interrupts the behavior, state or operation of the machine, and deletion of the log file" — [Rob Hulsebos, Draft EN 50742 Review v2, Jan 2026 (PDF)](https://www.enodenetworks.com/assets/docs/DraftEN50742Review.pdf); [regulatorydecoded.com](https://regulatorydecoded.com/how-pren-50742-is-redefining-machine-compliance/).
- Log properties: records created automatically "without human prompting"; "stored in a way that they cannot be deleted or altered by the person performing the intervention"; retention "at least five years"; at minimum "evidence of the last intervention of each type"; "Digital evidence of interventions shall be readable and accessible. The documentation shall provide information on how to access and interpret the recorded evidence"; timestamps for correlation, "timestamping can be provided by the device making the log event or the remote log collector" — [regulatorydecoded.com](https://regulatorydecoded.com/how-pren-50742-is-redefining-machine-compliance/); [Rob Hulsebos review (PDF)](https://www.enodenetworks.com/assets/docs/DraftEN50742Review.pdf); [cyber-regulation.com](https://cyber-regulation.com/article/en-50742-protection-against-machine-tampering).
- Academic use of the draft: a 2026 arXiv paper assesses IO-Link Wireless / OPC UA over 5G "under prEN 50742" — [arXiv 2607.15840](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15840).
- Training market already exists: exida runs "EN 50742 in Practice" (DE0613) — [exida](https://www.exida-eu.com/all-trainings/cybersecurity/457-cybersecurity-en50742-de0613); IBF runs a web seminar "Schutz gegen Korrumpierung nach Maschinenverordnung – die neue EN 50742" — [IBF](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/seminare-und-wissen/technische-spezialseminare/web-seminar-schutz-gegen-korrumpierung-nach-maschinenverordnung-die-neue-en-50742); maschinenrichtlinie.de hosts a 2026 Maschinenbautage workshop "Security im Rahmen der neuen EU-Maschinenverordnung" — [maschinenrichtlinie.de](https://www.maschinenrichtlinie.de/fortbildung/konferenzen/workshops-maschinenbautage-2026/security-im-rahmen-der-neuen-eu-maschinenverordnung/).
- KAN/DGUV analysis of the interplay between CRA, Machinery Regulation and standards (KANBrief 2/25, J. Stein & A. Sonnenburg) — [DGUV PDF](https://www.dguv.de/medien/ifa/de/fac/industrial-security/kanbrief-2025-2-j-stein-a-sonnenburg-combating-vulnerabilities-with-standards-new-eu-cybersecurity-rules.pdf) (content not read).

### Inferences
- A Modbus-write gateway that authenticates the origin of every order (unique per-node keys) and verifies payload integrity maps naturally to the SRSL1–SRSL3 "entities authenticated / uniquely authenticated" and "integrity of exchanged information" lines; a hash-chained log that the intervening person cannot alter, with timestamps and a documented format, maps to the draft's log-protection, correlation and "how to access and interpret" requirements. Secure boot is only mandatory at SRSL3, so an evidence pack can be positioned for SRSL1–2 machines without claiming secure boot.
- Because the standard is still FprEN with no confirmed OJ citation, a vendor cannot claim "EN 50742 compliant"; it can only claim coverage of the EHSR itself (1.1.9 / 1.2.1) with a traceability table to the draft's clauses, labelled "draft FprEN 50742:2026" (consistent with `prompts/OASIS_PILOT_GATEWAY.md`, which forbids claiming EN 50742 conformity).

### Gaps
- Formal vote closing date and result; expected publication date; CENELEC TC 44X project page (unreachable).
- Draft text on **boot counters** and on a *documented log format*: not mentioned in any summary found; the "boot counter" concept appears only in the user's premise. Treat as unconfirmed.
- Scope exclusion for machines placed on the market before publication: **not found** in any summary. Unconfirmed.
- Clause numbers for every requirement above (summaries did not give them).

## Q3. Postponement request (CEMA/CECIMO/Orgalim), ETUC opposition, Commission response, probability

### Takeaway
Industry (CECE, CECIMO, CEMA, EGMF, FEM and others) asked on 19–20 January 2026 to postpone 1.1.9 and 1.2.1(f) to the CRA date of 11 December 2027 (and AI provisions by ≥ 2 years); ETUC opposed it. No source found records a Commission decision; the most recent dated sources (June and August–September 2026) still treat 20 January 2027 as binding, and the Commission's 6 August 2026 answer to Parliament only acknowledges that the cyber/AI standards are not delivered. The Omnibus IV political deal of 9 June 2026 gave machine builders a 30-month transition for digital DoCs, not a cyber delay.

### Cited Findings
- Joint industry position of 19 January 2026: harmonised standards for the cyber requirements "not expected until late 2026", too little time; proposes moving the deadline to **December 2027** to match the CRA — [CEMA position paper (PDF)](https://www.cema-agri.org/images/publications/News/2026-01-19-Joint_industry_Position_on_cybersecurity_provisions_in_the_Machinery_Regulation.pdf); [CEMA news](https://www.cema-agri.org/publications/18-news-publications/1118-joint-industry-call-on-cybersecurity-and-the-machinery-regulation); names EHSR 1.1.9 and 1.2.1(f) as the main concern — [CECIMO](https://www.cecimo.eu/news/joint-industry-call-on-cybersecurity-and-machinery-regulation/).
- Signatories also include CECE, EGMF and FEM; request also covers AI-related provisions; proposal presented to the EC Machinery Expert Group (MEG) — [IBF postponement page](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/news-and-knowledge/technical-papers-and-news-on-ce-marking/postponement-of-mr-requirements-for-cybersecurity-and-ai).
- ETUC: "strongly opposes these proposed delays. Additional time cannot be granted at the expense of workers' safety"; the rules are already clear, notified bodies can be accredited without harmonised standards; "Making compliance dependent on the forthcoming European Commission Guide on machinery regulation risks indefinite postponement"; the requirement that a remotely accessible machine be free of vulnerabilities "is not new" — [ETUC paper (PDF, hosted by IBF)](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/fileadmin/dateidownloads/etuc-position-on-industry-paper.pdf); [CEN-CENELEC newsletter on ETUC "Don't Stop Me Now"](https://www.cencenelec.eu/news-events/news/2025/newsletter/ots-67-etuc/).
- Status: "final decision from the European Commission is still pending" (late 2025) — [Nemko guide](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/eu-machinery-regulation); postponement to the CRA date "bislang aber nicht beschlossen", 20 Jan 2027 remains applicable (update 23 June 2026) — [cyber-regulierung.de](https://www.cyber-regulierung.de/mvo-security-pflicht/).
- Commission answer to a parliamentary question, 6 August 2026: standards addressing cyber-safety and AI "have not yet been delivered"; Commission plans to re-cite most existing machinery harmonised standards in Q4 2026 — reported via [IBF status of harmonised standards](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/new-machinery-regulation-status-of-harmonised-standards) and [Foresight](https://www.useforesight.io/news/machinery-harmonised-standards-2027) (attribution per search summary; exact page not read).
- Omnibus IV: political agreement 9 June 2026; main gain for machine builders = **30-month transition period for digital Declarations of Conformity** (Fedecom/CEMA/Orgalim lobby) — [Fedecom](https://www.fedecom.nl/en/blog/news/joint-lobby-by-fedecom-cema-and-orgalim-yields-significant-gains-for-machine-builders-2/). February 2026 joint statement EGMF/FEM/CECE on the digital DoC exclusion — [Nemko guide](https://digital.nemko.com/regulations/eu-machinery-regulation).
- Commission Guide to the Machinery Regulation: status tracked by IBF — [IBF guide news](https://www.ibf-solutions.com/en/seminars-and-news/news/news-on-the-guide-for-the-machinery-regulation) (content not read).

### Inferences
- Probability of a postponement effective before 20 January 2027: **low** (my estimate ≤ 20 %). Reasoning: the date is in the Regulation itself and can only move by a legislative amendment; the one legislative vehicle that closed in 2026 (Omnibus IV, deal 9 June 2026) delivered a digital-DoC transition and, per the Fedecom summary, nothing on cyber; three months remain; ETUC and the Commission's own August 2026 answer show no appetite. A later Omnibus amendment in 2027 remains possible, which would convert the sales argument from "hard deadline" to "presumption-of-conformity gap" — still real, because without a cited harmonised standard a manufacturer must document 1.1.9 compliance by its own evidence.
- The "updated March 2026" industry request mentioned in the assignment was **not found**; it may exist on Orgalim's site (unreachable).

### Gaps
- Machinery Expert Group 2026 minutes; Orgalim's own position page; any Commission non-paper on 1.1.9. Unreachable/not found.
- Whether the Commission intends to issue guidance (rather than a delay) on how to demonstrate 1.1.9 without EN 50742.

## Q4. What notified bodies / consultants will expect as evidence; offers and prices

### Takeaway
No notified body or consultant publishes a price for machinery-cybersecurity assessment; offers exist (Pilz SEM/SAM, TÜV NORD and TÜV Rheinland conformity support, exida and IBF training) but the evidence they describe is generic (threat/attack-potential assessment, protection measures, intervention evidence, software identification). Day-rate benchmarks exist only from adjacent regimes: MDR notified bodies €2,290–€4,032/day; CRA Module B estimates €20k–€60k per engagement.

## Q7. Retrofit / integrator angle (who becomes manufacturer; evidence needed)

### Takeaway
A control-system modernisation that is "not foreseen or planned by the manufacturer" and creates a new hazard or raises a risk requiring a safety-control-system change makes the modifier a manufacturer (Art. 18 → Art. 10), so the retrofitter must assemble an Annex IV technical file including 1.1.9/1.2.1 evidence for the modified machine; the digital-means clause explicitly captures software/controls retrofits.

### Cited Findings
- Definition includes modification "by physical or digital means" and the trigger "addition of guards or protective devices… which necessitates the modification of the existing safety control system" — [EUR-Lex 2023/1230](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj/eng); explanations at [docks.io](https://docks.io/en/maschinenverordnung/wesentliche-veraenderung/), [eshield.pl](https://eshield.pl/en/knowledge/substantial-modification-machinery/), [GT Engineering](https://www.gt-engineering.it/en/insights/machinery-directive/machinery-substantial-modifications/).
- Modifier "shall be considered a manufacturer" and carries Article 10 obligations; commentators stress in-house retrofits can shift CE obligations — [getreadycompliance.eu](https://getreadycompliance.eu/substantial-modification-ce-marking/); [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj/eng).
- Machines placed on the market up to 19 January 2027 fall under Directive 2006/42/EC; from 20 January 2027 under the Regulation — [Checkmate Experts](https://www.checkmate.expert/en/maschinenverordnung-cybersicherheit.html); [TÜV Rheinland](https://www.tuv.com/world/en/new-machinery-regulation-eu-2023-1230.html).
- Belgian/Dutch consultancies already sell "cyber risk assessment for machines" to builders and operators — [D-SC](https://d-sc.nl/cyberrisicobeoordeling-voor-machines/); [Gain](https://www.gain.nl/cybersecurity-en-machineveiligheid-richting-2027/).

### Inferences
- A PLC/HMI replacement that adds remote maintenance or a Modbus TCP connection introduces exactly the "connection of another device / remote device" hazard that 1.1.9 §1 addresses; whether it is "substantial" turns on whether it creates a new hazard requiring a safety-control-system change — a retrofitter adding networked actuation will often meet that test and would then need a 1.1.9 evidence set for the whole machine, not just the new controller. The gateway + log pack fits this buyer because it is the only part of an old machine's control chain the retrofitter fully owns.
- Retrofit integrators (system integrators, panel builders, "revamping" firms) are not identified by name here — see Gaps.
