# Competitive landscape — authorization/filtering of write commands to industrial devices (Modbus TCP/RTU and similar) and tamper-evident intervention logs, as of 9 October 2026

Research notes for the OASIS positioning work. Method: 25 WebSearch queries (standard + extended) on 9 Oct 2026; **direct page fetches were impossible from this sandbox** (WebFetch DNS failures, proxy 403 on CONNECT to ietf.org, veridify.com, packetviper.com, pv-magazine.com, belden.com, elmark-automation.com, industrialcyber.co), and the shared search budget ran out before the last 7 planned queries. Consequence: every finding below rests on search-result snippets and summaries of the linked pages, not on full-text reads. Facts I could not source in-session are listed under *Gaps*, never asserted. Reference-product facts are cited to the repo (`/home/user/oasis/CLAUDE.md`, `docs/specs/*`).

Vocabulary used throughout:
- **Network allowlisting / DPI**: the device decides on IP/port/function-code/register/value and (sometimes) a device-side session; the *command itself carries no cryptographic proof of who authorized it*.
- **Cryptographic per-command authorization**: each write carries a signature (or MAC) that binds the authorizer's identity to that command's payload, with freshness, so a relay or a compromised network cannot forge/replay it.

## KQ1 — Competitor matrix: who does what, in which form factor, and what is documented vs claimed

### Takeaway
As of 9 Oct 2026, every incumbent inline product documented in this session (Belden/Hirschmann EAGLE40, Phoenix Contact mGuard Modbus Inspector, Moxa EDR-G9010, PacketViper Modbus Command Protection, Siemens SCALANCE S / SC-600 family, Fortinet FortiGate Rugged, TXOne EdgeIPS/vEdgeIPS Pro) works by **network/DPI allowlisting** (device, function code, register, sometimes value), not by a per-command signature bound to the payload. Only three documented entities are in OASIS's cryptographic lane: **Veridify DOME Sentry** (mutual device authentication + encrypted tunnels; the per-command claim is marketing language, protocol not published), **Solarsecure Tech's SolarSecure Vision** (German pre-PoC gateway that "passes only instructions it can classify as safe or verify through cryptographic authentication"; no technical disclosure), and the **IETF individual draft draft-morrison-ot-command-authority-02** (a specification with no interoperable implementation). No product found documents *logging refused commands in a hash chain*, *boot-id freshness*, *k-of-n operator quorum*, or *formal verification of the decision rule*.

## KQ2 — Standards, drafts, academic and open-source work relevant to signed OT commands and refusal logging

### Takeaway
There is active specification work but no deployed standard for *operator-level, per-command* authorization on Modbus: Modbus/TCP Security (TLS) exists since 2018 but "never really saw adoption"; IEEE 2030.5/CSIP is the strongest DER security profile yet still endpoint-level; the only text that resembles OASIS's envelope — IETF individual draft `draft-morrison-ot-command-authority-02` (31 Aug 2026, Alter Meridian Pty Ltd) — has "no interoperable implementations". Academic work (Messina/Bologna 2025) proposes PQC + SSI proxies for legacy Modbus, validating the proxy architecture OASIS uses.

- **Unique (documented, as of 9 Oct 2026)**: (1) cryptographic binding of operator identity to each write's payload with freshness tied to the actuator's boot-id; (2) refusal reasons as first-class, ordered, persisted records (`NotAuthorized`, `Rollback`, `HashMismatch`, R14-unsafe) — the intervention log covers *attempted* interventions, which change-management tools (octoplant/AutoSave) structurally cannot see; (3) k-of-n operator quorum for revocation/policy; (4) formal verification of the gate; (5) `no_std` MCU footprint enabling a sub-€50 BOM DIN-rail device (BOM inference, not sourced).
- **Loses**: certification (no 62443-4-2, no BSI/VDE/ANSSI), audit, references, protocol coverage (no FC03/04 reads, no FC05/15 coils, no DNP3/IEC-104/S7/EtherNet-IP), no network-facing Modbus TCP binary yet, no signed register map, single-device, TTL not RS-485, no secure element, no rate limit. Against Waterfall-style buyers who want *no* inbound writes at all, OASIS's value proposition (authorized writes) is orthogonal and may be rejected on policy grounds.
- **Positioning risk**: Veridify can truthfully say "all commands originate from an authenticated source" and "blocked attempts are logged"; OASIS's differentiation must be stated at the level of *what is in the signature and what is in the log*, with Veridify's internals marked "not documented".

### Gaps
- Independent latency/throughput comparisons (OASIS has K=10 bands; competitors publish RFC 2544 pps only) — no like-for-like public benchmark exists.
- Whether any competitor's log is signed/hash-chained (not documented anywhere retrieved — absence of evidence, not evidence of absence).

## KQ4 — Which competitors are likely to add signed-command features soon

### Takeaway
Three signals point to near-term movement: Schneider–SECLAB promised a "new generation of OT-specific products" for 2027 (application-level PLC/DCS/SIS filtering, no signature mentioned); Solarsecure Tech plans BSI certification of a cryptographically-verifying inverter gateway; and an IETF individual draft (Aug 2026) now specifies attributable command authority, with reference ports in four languages. Among incumbents, Rockwell (CIP Security proxy) and Veridify (device root of trust) already hold the cryptographic building blocks closest to per-command signing; the DPI vendors (Moxa, Hirschmann, Phoenix, TXOne, PacketViper) show no documented sign of moving beyond allowlisting.

### Cited Findings
- Schneider–SECLAB: "plan to develop a new generation of OT-specific products expected in 2027", CRA/NIS2 framing — [Industrial Cyber](https://industrialcyber.co/news/schneider-electric-seclab-expand-ot-cybersecurity-collaboration-for-industrial-infrastructure/), [IT Nerd](https://itnerd.blog/2026/09/29/schneider-electric-seclab-expand-ot-security-partnership/).
- Solarsecure Tech: BSI + industry-association certification planned; PoC projects in preparation (Feb 2026) — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/02/25/german-startup-launches-gateway-to-block-inverter-kill-switches/).
- IETF draft -02 with JS/Python/Go reference ports and an external Rust port; no interoperable implementations yet — [IETF](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-morrison-ot-command-authority-02.html).
- Rockwell CIP Security Proxy brings authenticity/integrity to legacy EtherNet/IP devices — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201119005490/en).
- Veridify: devices "generate their own root of trust", DIA handles credentials and logging — [Veridify](https://www.veridify.com/dome/), [Veridify smart buildings](https://www.veridify.com/article/applying-zero-trust-to-ot-networks-for-smart-buildings/).
- TXOne Sept 2026 release direction is AI policy automation + virtual appliances, not cryptographic command authority — [TXOne](https://www.txone.com/news/txone-ai-edge-launch-vedgeips-pro-semicon-tw-2026/).
- PacketViper July 2026 direction is inline per-device Modbus policy via moving-target defense — [press release](https://www.mycarrollcountynews.com/online_features/press_releases/article_c169fbb0-a208-5438-b20f-5882b4edb0d1.html).
- Regulatory pull cited by vendors: CRA reporting from Sept 2026 (Device Authority framing) — [KeyScaler 2025.1](https://deviceauthority.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/KeyScaler-2025.1.pdf); NIS2 for PV operators — [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/03/18/how-eu-nis2-is-reshaping-cybersecurity-for-pv-plant-operators/).

### Inferences
- Most likely to ship something adjacent to signed commands within 12-18 months: **Schneider/SECLAB** (2027 product line, Schneider owns the Modbus trademark and the Modicon installed base), **Veridify** (already sells authenticated-source retrofit, would need only to expose per-command semantics and a verifiable log), **Solarsecure** (if its PoCs convert). Less likely: pure DPI vendors, whose roadmaps (AI policy generation, virtualization) deepen allowlisting rather than replace it.
- The IETF draft lowers the cost for any vendor to adopt an envelope format; OASIS's best defensive move is to publish its own envelope/test vectors and the refusal-log format before a competitor anchors the de-facto standard.

### Gaps
- No vendor roadmap document mentioning "signed commands", "command signature" or "per-write authorization" was retrieved for any incumbent; this section is inference from direction-of-travel, not from announcements.
- Claroty/Nozomi/Dragos 2026 product directions (possible active-response or write-blocking features) not researched (budget).
